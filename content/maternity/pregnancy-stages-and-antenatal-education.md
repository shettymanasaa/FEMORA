# Pregnancy Stages and Antenatal Care Milestones

A full-term pregnancy averages 40 weeks calculated from the first day of your last menstrual period (LMP). Healthcare providers track progress across three distinct trimesters.

## Trimester Breakdown

### First Trimester (Weeks 1 to 13)
- **Fetal Development:** Rapid embryogenesis occurs. By week 8, major organs, limb buds, neural tube, and early heart structures are formed. By week 12, the fetus moves limbs and possesses distinct facial features.
- **Maternal Body:** High levels of human chorionic gonadotropin (hCG) and progesterone promote uterine lining support. Symptoms often include nausea ("morning sickness"), fatigue, heightened smell sensitivity, and breast enlargement.
- **Key Check-ups:** Initial booking visit, routine maternal blood and urine labs, and dating ultrasound (often around weeks 10–13).

### Second Trimester (Weeks 14 to 27)
- **Fetal Development:** Skeleton begins to ossify, hearing develops, and fine lanugo hair covers the skin. By week 20, quickening (maternal perception of fetal movements) typically begins.
- **Maternal Body:** Often considered the "golden trimester" as early nausea subsides and energy returns. The expanding uterus elevates into the abdomen. Round ligament aches and mild pedal edema may emerge.
- **Key Check-ups:** Detailed mid-pregnancy anatomy scan (typically between 18 and 22 weeks), gestational diabetes screening (24–28 weeks).

### Third Trimester (Weeks 28 to 40+)
- **Fetal Development:** Rapid weight gain, fat deposition, and pulmonary surfactant production. The brain and lungs reach maturity. The baby typically turns into a cephalic (head-down) position.
- **Maternal Body:** The gravid uterus exerts pressure on the diaphragm, stomach, and bladder, causing shortness of breath, heartburn, and urinary frequency. Braxton Hicks contractions may occur irregularly.
- **Key Check-ups:** Antenatal visits increase in frequency (every 2 weeks, then weekly from 36 weeks onwards) to assess blood pressure, fetal heart rate, fundal height, and presentation.
