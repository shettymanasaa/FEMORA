# Postpartum Mental Health: Baby Blues, PPD, and Emotional Care

Welcoming a newborn brings immense emotional transitions alongside sudden drops in estrogen and progesterone and profound sleep disruption.

## Distinguishing Between Conditions
- **The "Baby Blues" (Normal & Transient):**
  - Occurs in up to 80% of mothers, typically starting between days 2 and 5 postpartum and resolving naturally within 10 to 14 days.
  - Manifests as mood swings, tearfulness, anxiety, and mild irritability. Responsive to sleep, partner reassurance, and practical household help.
- **Postpartum Depression (PPD):**
  - Affects approximately 15% of women and can develop anytime in the first year postpartum.
  - Symptoms persist beyond two weeks and include pervasive sadness, feelings of inadequacy or guilt, inability to bond with the baby, persistent fatigue, and loss of appetite or sleep even when the baby sleeps.
  - PPD is a treatable medical condition, responsive to counseling, support networks, and lactation-compatible medications.
- **Emergency: Postpartum Psychosis (Urgent Psychiatric Evaluation Required):**
  - A rare (1 to 2 in 1,000) but severe psychiatric emergency marked by confusion, rapid mood swings, hallucinations, paranoia, insomnia, or irrational thoughts. Seek emergency hospital intervention immediately.
