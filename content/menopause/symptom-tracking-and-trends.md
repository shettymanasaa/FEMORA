# Symptom Tracking and Trend Analysis in Menopause Care

Because hormone levels fluctuate widely during perimenopause, single blood hormone tests (such as FSH or estradiol) are rarely helpful in diagnosing the transition or determining management. Longitudinal symptom tracking provides far more reliable clinical insight.

## Why Daily Tracking Matters
- **Identifies Environmental Triggers:** Tracking reveals whether hot flashes correlate with evening alcohol, hot beverages, spicy seasonings, temperature spikes, or workplace stress.
- **Quantifies Quality of Life Impact:** Clinicians evaluate the frequency (e.g., flashes per day), intensity (mild, moderate, severe), and sleep interference when considering medical therapy.
- **Evaluates Therapeutic Response:** When initiating Menopausal Hormone Therapy (MHT) or non-hormonal treatments, a recorded baseline enables you and your doctor to evaluate symptom reduction over 8 to 12 weeks.

## Practical Tracking Guidelines
1. Log vasomotor episodes (hot flashes and night sweats) with a simple count or severity score.
2. Record nightly sleep quality and waking fatigue.
3. Note vaginal or urinary sensations (e.g., dryness, burning, urgency).
4. Review your weekly and monthly summaries before scheduling your consultation.
