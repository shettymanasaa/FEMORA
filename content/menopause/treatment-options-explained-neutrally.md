# Menopause Management: Evidence-Based Treatment Options Explained Neutrally

Choosing how to navigate menopausal symptoms is an individualized decision made in collaboration with a qualified clinician. Treatments range from lifestyle interventions to prescription medications.

## 1. Menopausal Hormone Therapy (MHT / HRT)
- **What It Is:** Replacement of estrogen (often paired with a progestogen for women with an intact uterus to protect the endometrial lining against hyperplasia).
- **Administration Routes:** Oral tablets, transdermal skin patches, topical gels, and sprays. Transdermal preparations bypass hepatic first-pass metabolism and carry a lower risk of venous thromboembolism (blood clots) than oral forms.
- **Evidence-Based Benefits:** The most effective clinical therapy for severe hot flashes, night sweats, sleep restoration, and prevention of postmenopausal bone loss and osteoporotic fractures.
- **Risks & Considerations:** Individual risk profiles depend on age, years since menopause onset, and medical history. Contraindications include personal history of breast cancer, active coronary artery disease, unexplained uterine bleeding, or active thromboembolic disease.

## 2. Non-Hormonal Prescription Therapies
- **Selective Serotonin/Norepinephrine Reuptake Inhibitors (SSRIs/SNRIs):** Low-dose medications (e.g., paroxetine, venlafaxine) modulate thermoregulatory neurotransmitters in the hypothalamus and reduce hot flash severity by 50–65%.
- **Neurokinin 3 (NK3) Receptor Antagonists (e.g., Fezolinetant):** Non-hormonal targeted molecules that block NKB receptor binding on thermoregulatory neurons, reducing moderate-to-severe vasomotor flashes without systemic hormonal effects.
- **Gabapentin:** An anticonvulsant that can reduce nocturnal vasomotor symptoms and assist sleep architecture.

## 3. Local (Vaginal) Estrogen Therapies
- Low-dose vaginal creams, rings, or dissolving tablets applied locally. Because systemic absorption is minimal, systemic progestogen is generally not required. Highly effective for vaginal dryness, pain during intercourse, and recurrent postmenopausal UTIs.

## 4. Behavioral & Non-Pharmacological Strategies
- **Cognitive Behavioral Therapy (CBT):** Validated in clinical trials to improve psychological coping and reduce the perceived distress of hot flashes.
- **Acupuncture & Hypnosis:** Clinical studies indicate moderate improvements in subjective symptom burden.
