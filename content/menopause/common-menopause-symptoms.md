# Common Menopausal Symptoms and Vasomotor Changes

Estrogen receptors are located throughout the brain, blood vessels, bone, skin, and pelvic tissues, explaining why menopausal symptoms can be diverse and multifaceted.

## Primary Symptom Clusters
- **Vasomotor Symptoms (Hot Flashes & Night Sweats):** A sudden sensation of intense heat spreading across the chest, neck, and face, often accompanied by palpitations, sweating, and subsequent chills. Caused by hypothalamic thermoregulatory instability as neurokinin B (NKB) signaling increases.
- **Sleep Disruption:** May be secondary to nighttime vasomotor surges (drenching night sweats) or due to changes in melatonin rhythms and progesterone decline.
- **Cognitive and Mood Fluctuations:** Word-finding difficulties, short-term forgetfulness ("brain fog"), irritability, or heightened anxiety. Studies confirm these symptoms are common, physiological, and often stabilize in postmenopause.
- **Genitourinary Syndrome of Menopause (GSM):** Thinning and reduced elasticity of the vaginal epithelium (*atrophy*), dryness, burning, dyspareunia, and increased frequency of urinary tract infections (UTIs). Unlike hot flashes, GSM symptoms typically do not resolve spontaneously without local treatment.
- **Musculoskeletal Aches:** Estrogen exerts anti-inflammatory effects on cartilage; its decline frequently coincides with morning joint stiffness and myalgia.
