# When to Seek Medical Care for Menstrual Symptoms

While moderate discomfort and minor schedule shifts can occur naturally, certain symptoms warrant professional medical attention to ensure your well-being.

## Urgent Warning Signs (Seek Immediate Medical Care)
Please go to an emergency room or contact urgent medical services immediately if you experience:
- **Excessive, Rapid Blood Loss:** Soaking through two or more heavy pads or tampons every hour for 2 consecutive hours.
- **Signs of Acute Hypovolemia/Anemia:** Dizziness, lightheadedness, sudden pale skin, fainting, shortness of breath, or rapid heartbeat.
- **Severe, Sudden Pelvic Pain:** Severe acute pelvic or abdominal pain, especially if accompanied by high fever, vomiting, or chills.
- **Possible Pregnancy Complication:** Severe unilateral pelvic cramping or heavy bleeding if pregnancy is possible (to rule out ectopic pregnancy).

## Non-Urgent Indicators for Clinical Consultation
Schedule a consultation with your gynecologist or primary care physician if you notice:
- **Prolonged Bleeding:** Periods regularly lasting longer than 7 to 8 days.
- **Irregular Intervals:** Cycles consistently shorter than 21 days or longer than 35 days, or sudden cessation of periods for 3+ months (secondary amenorrhea).
- **Intermenstrual or Post-coital Bleeding:** Bleeding or spotting between periods or following sexual intercourse.
- **Debilitating Pain:** Period pain that prevents you from going to work, school, or daily activities, or does not respond to standard pain relievers.
- **Large Blood Clots:** Consistently passing blood clots larger than 2.5 cm (approximately the size of a quarter).
