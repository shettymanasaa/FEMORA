# Common Menstrual Symptoms and Physiology

Menstrual symptoms vary across individuals and even across cycles. Understanding the biological drivers helps in managing them effectively.

## Common Symptoms
- **Uterine Cramping (Dysmenorrhea):** Triggered by prostaglandins—hormone-like lipids produced in the endometrium that stimulate uterine muscle contractions. Higher prostaglandin levels correspond to sharper cramping.
- **Lower Back & Thigh Aches:** Pain signals from uterine contraction can radiate along pelvic nerve pathways to the sacrum and upper thighs.
- **Bloating & Gastrointestinal Changes:** Progesterone relaxes smooth muscle, while prostaglandins can affect bowel contractility, sometimes causing loose stools or nausea in the first 48 hours.
- **Headaches & Fatigue:** Driven by rapid drops in circulating estrogen immediately prior to or at the start of menstruation.
- **Breast Tenderness (Mastalgia):** Responsive to cyclic estrogen stimulation and progesterone-induced fluid retention in glandular breast tissue.

## Self-Care and Comfort Strategies
- **Heat Application:** Applying a heating pad or hot water bottle to the lower abdomen relaxes the myometrium (uterine muscle) and improves local blood flow.
- **Hydration & Electrolytes:** Maintaining adequate fluid intake helps counter fluid retention and reduces fatigue.
- **Gentle Movement:** Low-intensity walking, stretching, or yoga can stimulate endorphin release, acting as a natural analgesic.
