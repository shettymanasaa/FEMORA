# Period Tracking and Irregular Menstrual Cycles

Tracking your cycle establishes what is normal for you and provides objective information for your healthcare provider.

## What is Considered a Regular Cycle?
- **Cycle Frequency:** 21 to 35 days (from Day 1 of one period to Day 1 of the subsequent period). In adolescent years, cycles may range up to 45 days.
- **Cycle Regularity:** Cycle-to-cycle variation of less than 7 to 9 days across a 12-month span.
- **Bleeding Duration:** 3 to 7 days of active flow.

## Common Causes of Irregularity
1. **Life Transitions:** Cycles are naturally more variable in the first few years after menarche (first period) and during perimenopause due to fluctuating ovulation patterns.
2. **Stress & Cortisol:** Elevated physiological or emotional stress alters hypothalamic gonadotropin-releasing hormone (GnRH) signaling, delaying or inhibiting ovulation.
3. **Weight Fluctuations & High Intensity Training:** Significant caloric deficits or rapid body composition changes can affect hormone production.
4. **Endocrine Conditions:** Polycystic Ovary Syndrome (PCOS) and thyroid disorders (hypo- or hyperthyroidism) are common clinical causes of irregular ovulation.
5. **Medication & Contraception:** Starting, stopping, or using hormonal contraceptives or emergency contraception frequently changes flow patterns.

## How Tracking Helps
Recording the first day of bleeding, flow volume, and daily symptoms creates a longitudinal trend summary. Instead of guessing during clinical appointments, you can share concrete counts and cycle intervals.
