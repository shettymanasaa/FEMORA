# Managing Pain, Fatigue, Sleep, and Mood

Many women experience cyclical changes in energy, emotional resilience, and physical sensation. Differentiating common fluctuations from severe functional limitations is key to appropriate care.

## Primary vs. Secondary Dysmenorrhea
- **Primary Dysmenorrhea:** Common period cramps caused by normal endometrial prostaglandin release. They typically begin within 1 to 2 years after menarche and improve over 48 to 72 hours.
- **Secondary Dysmenorrhea:** Menstrual pain related to identifiable pelvic conditions, such as endometriosis, adenomyosis, or uterine fibroids. Pain may begin days before bleeding starts, persist throughout the cycle, or fail to respond to standard anti-inflammatory medications.

## Sleep and Cyclical Mood Changes
- Progesterone metabolites interact with GABA receptors in the central nervous system. As progesterone peaks and drops in the luteal phase, some individuals experience heightened anxiety, emotional sensitivity, or sleep fragmentation.
- **Sleep Hygiene:** Keeping a consistent sleep schedule and limiting caffeine and screen time in the luteal phase can support circadian stability.
- **Nutritional Support:** Ensuring adequate dietary iron is especially important for individuals with heavy bleeding to prevent fatigue related to iron depletion or anemia.
