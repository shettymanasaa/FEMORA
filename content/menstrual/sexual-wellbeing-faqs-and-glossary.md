# Sexual Wellbeing FAQs and Clinical Glossary

> **Notice:** This document is currently marked as **Pending Clinical Review**. Information here provides general reference concepts drafted by the team, pending formal independent clinical panel verification.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Can pregnancy occur from intercourse during menstruation?
While unlikely, pregnancy is biologically possible, particularly for women with shorter cycles (e.g., 21 to 24 days) where ovulation may occur soon after menses, combined with sperm viability in reproductive tract secretions for up to 5 days.

### What causes spotting after intimacy?
Post-coital spotting can arise from cervical friction, cervical polyps, ectropion (where delicate cells line the outer cervix), micro-tears from insufficient lubrication, or low-grade infection. Recurrent post-coital bleeding requires clinical examination by a doctor.

### Is it normal to experience discomfort when inserting tampons or menstrual cups?
Mild difficulty is common during early attempts due to pelvic floor tension or improper angling. However, recurrent pain or inability to insert menstrual products or engage in penetration may suggest vaginismus or anatomical sensitivity that can be treated with pelvic floor physiotherapy.

## Clinical Glossary
- **Dyspareunia:** Persistent or recurrent pain experienced before, during, or after sexual intercourse.
- **Cervical Ectropion:** A benign condition where glandular cells from inside the cervical canal turn outward to the vaginal part of the cervix, making it prone to bleed easily upon touch.
- **Pelvic Floor Hypertonicity:** Excessive muscle tension in the pelvic diaphragm that can contribute to pain during penetration, urinary urgency, and chronic pelvic discomfort.
- **Bacterial Vaginosis (BV):** An imbalance of the normal vaginal microflora with reduced *Lactobacillus* and increased anaerobic organisms, frequently presenting with thin watery discharge and a distinctive odor.
